Manim · in-call devices · interaction study

The island should own its picker, not hand it to the stage.

Tapping the mic selector on a phone opens a popover full of nested dropdowns, and four seconds later the bar it was attached to slides away and leaves it there. The orphaning is fixed. This is about the pattern underneath it — and none of Meet, Teams, Zoom, WhatsApp or FaceTime uses a dropdown here.

The one job
“Play the audio somewhere else.” On a phone, device selection is almost always routing — earpiece, speaker, the headset that just connected.
Hard constraint
Pure touch. No Esc, no hover. Everything closes by an X, a tap on the trigger, or a drag down.
Hard constraint
No page scroll. Panels scroll internally. Check mobile-sm (375×667).
Already fixed
The orphaned island and the leaked desktop carets — see the audit. This study replaces the pattern, not the bug.

What it does today

Two states, both real. On the left, the caret that was never meant to be on a phone (hidden pointer-fine:inline-flex is inert on an IconButton) opening a side="top" popover whose rows each open another popover. Radix flips a panel that doesn't fit, so on a short phone the second one can genuinely resolve downward — a dropdown hanging off a control that sits 40 px from the bottom of the screen.

On the right, the orphaning: the same popover four seconds later. The countdown was armed on mount and reset only by taps on the stage, so a picker opened at t=3.9s had 100 ms to live, and tapping the bar bought it nothing.

Manim already gets the camera half right — flip lives on the self-view tile, and the camera caret that duplicated it as a list is gone. What's left is the audio half.

A

Audio sheet

ref: meet · teams · zoom

The speaker button opens a bottom sheet from the same edge the island lives on. One flat level — no dropdown inside a popover inside a bar — with 56 px rows, routing first and the input/processing controls as a second section. The island stays exactly where it was, visible above the sheet's shoulder, so the thing you tapped is still on screen and still says it's open.

Route names come first because that's the decision (“put it on the headset”), with the device string as the secondary line for the machines that have five of them.

  • forMatches every reference app, and the sheet primitive already exists (Sheet side="bottom") with drag detents and a Close X.
  • forFlat. Nothing nests, so nothing can flip the wrong way or clip without a scroll container.
  • againstThe island is still a separate element that could drift from the sheet; it relies on the hold, not on structure.

B

The island grows a tray

ref: ios control centre · dynamic island

The persistent anchor taken literally: the island doesn't open a panel, it becomes one. The bar keeps its position and its buttons and gains a body above them, so the control bar is the tray's bottom row. There is no second element to lose track of — orphaning stops being a bug you can fix and becomes a state that cannot be constructed.

A segmented header carries Audio / Video, which is also where the two device carets we just deleted go: one tap to the same content, at a full-width target instead of a 26 px chevron.

  • forStructurally cannot orphan. The anchor is the surface.
  • forKeeps the island's identity — it grows in place instead of being covered by something that arrives from off-screen.
  • forMute / leave stay live and in the same place while you're picking a route, which the scrimmed sheet in A takes away.
  • againstA new primitive. Island has no expanding variant, and the height animation has to not reflow the stage.
  • againstLess room than a sheet — a long device list needs its own internal scroll, where the sheet can just grow a detent.

C

Route rail, in place

ref: whatsapp speaker toggle

The observation behind this one: the common case isn't “choose from my devices”, it's “not this one” — two or three routes, one of which is already correct. So the speaker button expands the island itself into a horizontal rail of route chips. No overlay, no scrim, nothing to dismiss: tap a chip and the rail collapses back to a button showing the route you're on.

The long tail (a specific mic on a laptop, noise suppression) doesn't disappear — the last chip is More, which opens A's sheet.

  • forThe fastest possible version of the common case, and it never covers the call.
  • forNo new surface at all — the island already reflows for the docked panel.
  • againstThe bar's buttons move sideways to make room, which is exactly the kind of shifting target the audit was complaining about.
  • againstOnly fits three or four chips at 375 px. Beyond that it's a scroller inside a control bar.

What all three assume

These are the moves that make any of them work, and they're where most of the value is. Two of them close findings the audit left open.

  • 01Routes, not device strings. “AirPods Pro · Bluetooth” beats “Default — Communications — AirPods Pro (Bluetooth)”. Group by what the user is choosing between; the raw label is the second line, for the machines that need it.
  • 02Nothing nests. One level, always. Every current mobile path is a picker inside a picker, and that's what produces both the flip and the clipping.
  • 03Don't offer routing iOS Safari can't do (audit F5). It exposes no audiooutput devices and no setSinkId, so a control labelled “Audio output” opens a panel with no output in it. Either hide it or call it “Audio” and let it carry the mic and noise controls it actually has.
  • 0444 px minimum on every touch row (audit F6). MenuRow and DropdownItem are ~36 px and the View chips ~32 px, on a surface that only exists for thumbs. All clear WCAG AA, none clear the platform guidance, and GridTile next to them is 68 px.
  • 05Camera stays a flip, not a list. Every reference app agrees, the self-view tile already has the button, and the caret that duplicated it as a list is gone.
  • 06Re-run the gates. test:mobile and test:mobile-sm for the no-scroll rule, and test:a11y in both themes — B introduces a new surface that holds focusable controls while the stage is still live, which is the exact shape that produced the aria-hidden-focus finding behind DropdownMenu's non-modal note.

If you want a recommendation

B for the shell, A's content model inside it, C's rail as the collapsed state. They compose rather than compete. B is the only one that answers the actual complaint structurally — a tray whose bottom row is the control bar cannot be separated from it, so the orphaned state stops being something a future control can reintroduce. A supplies what goes in it: one flat level, routes first, 56 px rows. C supplies the closed state — a speaker button that reads “AirPods” instead of a generic icon answers “where is my audio going?” without opening anything at all.

If only one thing ships, ship A. It's the smallest change, it uses a primitive that already exists with drag detents and a Close X, and it removes the nesting that causes the flip and the clipping. B's tray is the better answer and the one worth building, but it's a new primitive and it wants the a11y pass repeated.

C alone is tempting and I'd argue against it as the only change: it shifts the positions of the buttons either side of it, and a control bar whose targets move while you're reaching for them is the same class of problem as one that slides away.
