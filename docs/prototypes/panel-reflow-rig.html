Desktop layout · prototype for sign-off · 2026-08-21

The chat button hands you the Leave button.

Opening the chat or people panel re-centres the floating control bar in the remaining space. The bar slides left by half the panel's width — and the Leave control is sitting almost exactly that far to the right of the Chat button that triggered it. Click chat, don't move your mouse, click again to close it: you have left the call. This rig measures the collision and lets you feel four candidate fixes against it.

The measurement

It is not an edge case. It reproduces at every desktop breakpoint.

Both the stage and the control bar carry panel && 'md:pr-[20rem] lg:pr-[22rem] xl:pr-[25rem]' — RoomView.tsx:448 and ControlBar.tsx:516. On the bar that padding lands inside a fixed inset-x-0 flex justify-center container, so the bar's centre travels left by exactly half of it.

Four ways out

Options, with what each one costs.

All four keep the docked-and-reflowing panel that STYLE.md §4 calls for. They differ in what is allowed to move, and in whether safety comes from geometry or from a rule.

01

Move the bar only when it would actually collide

Recommended

The bar stops re-centring. It keeps its viewport centre and slides left only by the overlap it genuinely has with the panel — which on a 1440px screen is zero, because the bar measures 560px and there is 1044px of room. Travel drops from 200px to 0px at 1440, and to 48px at 1280.

Buys
The bar stops moving entirely on the screens most people are on. Nothing about the visual design changes.
Doesn't fix
768–1023px, where the bar and a 304px panel genuinely don't both fit: overlap is 224px and still lands on Leave.
Cost
One measured value instead of a static class — the bar's width has to be observed, so it survives a control being added.
02

Put nothing destructive downstream of a trigger

Keep the current reflow, and make the collision harmless: move the Leave cluster to the left end of the bar so a leftward slide can only ever bring a benign control under the pointer. The hazard becomes structurally impossible rather than numerically avoided.

Buys
Total immunity at every width, including 768px. Pure markup reorder, no new logic.
Breaks
Leave-on-the-right is the convention in Meet, Zoom, Teams and WhatsApp — worth a Mobbin check before committing. Existing users' muscle memory moves.
Cost
Re-teaches the most consequential button in the product to fix a bug most users never articulate.
03

Destructive controls ignore a click they didn't earn

Recommended, with 01

After any layout change moves a control under a pointer that hasn't moved, Leave and End-for-everyone refuse pointer activation until the pointer moves ~8px or ~350ms passes. The button never becomes visually disabled — the guard only rejects the one click nobody meant to aim. Keyboard and assistive tech are untouched.

Buys
Covers this reflow and every future one — a new control on the bar can't quietly re-open the hazard.
Watch
A genuinely fast, deliberate second click gets swallowed. 350ms is a guess and wants a real number.
Cost
A small shared hook, and one invariant the team has to keep honouring.
04

The grid reflows; the bar never does

Closest to the literal brief: the stage narrows and the tiles scale, and the control bar stays put on the viewport's centre line. Zero travel, always.

Buys
Nothing moves under the pointer, ever. One deleted class.
Breaks
At 1280 and below, the bar's right end runs under the panel — including, at 768px, the Leave button itself. Load it in the rig at 1024 and watch the panel swallow it.
Cost
Only viable if the panel goes overlay below lg, or the bar compacts.
The grid hypothesis

The grid already does the thing the brief hoped for.

The ask was worth testing whether the grid could scale tiles down, holding aspect, and wrap as needed instead of hard-shifting. It already does. fitMixedRows in Stage.tsx is a justified packer: it tries every row count, scales each row to fill the width, keeps each tile's snapped aspect, and picks the arrangement that maximises the smallest tile. Tile sizes are written as inline pixel width/height, and useElementSize re-measures through the 220ms transition — with a 2px threshold specifically to stop that becoming per-frame jank.

So the stage is not where the exit risk comes from. But the sweep did turn up one real grid defect next door:

Found while measuring

At 1024–1279px, opening the panel takes gridCapacity from 4 columns to 3 and page capacity from 16 to 12. With 16 in the call, four people vanish to page 2 the moment you open chat — not scaled down, gone. At 768px it is 12 tiles down to 8. Set the rig to 1024px with 16 in the call and toggle the panel.

Fix is independent of everything above: derive page capacity from the undocked stage width, or hold capacity across a panel toggle, so a docking panel can only ever shrink tiles — never repaginate them.

What I'd like signed off

01 and 03 together, plus the capacity hold.

01 removes the movement wherever removing it is free, which is most real desktops. 03 makes the remaining cases — 768–1023px, and any future control added to the bar — safe by rule rather than by a margin that happens to hold today. At 1024px option 01 lands the pointer 23px clear of the Leave control; one more button on the bar closes that gap. Twenty-three pixels is not a margin worth resting a call-ending action on, and that is the whole argument for pairing the two.

Three things I'd like a decision on before I write any of it:

  • 01 + 03, or 02? Option 02 is the only single change that is immune at 768px, and it costs the leave-on-the-right convention. That is a product call, not an engineering one.
  • 768–1023px. A 558px bar and a 304px docked panel do not fit in 768px of screen. Should the panel go overlay below lg, or should the bar compact — fold reactions and share into More?
  • The 350ms guard window. Worth a number from real use rather than my guess.

Nothing here is implemented — src/ is untouched by design, per prototype-first. Say which way and I'll build it behind the usual gates.
