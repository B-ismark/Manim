Manim · in-call stage · mobile layout study

A phone is not a narrow desktop, and the stage still treats it like one.

Measured at 375×667, the four scenarios fail in four different ways: a shared screen gets 31% of the display and 118 px of black bars, the speaker filmstrip spends most of its height underneath the control island, and the gallery packer was rendering people at two different sizes in the same call. Benchmark, numbers, then prototypes for each.

What the reference apps do on a phone

The interesting agreement isn't the grid — it's what they refuse to do. Nobody splits a portrait screen between a share and a roster. Nobody puts a persistent filmstrip on a phone in speaker view. Everybody pages the gallery by swipe with dots, and everybody keeps the self-view as a floating card rather than a grid cell.

Why the share is letterboxed. bigFraction is adaptive and correct: it sizes the big region to the content's natural need, which for a 16:9 share in a 359-wide portrait column is 0.310 of the height. Then MIN_FRACTION = 0.5 clamps it up. That floor is right on a desktop, where it stops the big region becoming a sliver beside a wide grid — but in a vertical split it forces 118 px of black bars above and below the share and hands the surplus to a roster nobody opened the call to look at.

Why nothing caught the filmstrip collision. tests/19-overlays uses the overlaps() helper, which selects button, a, input, select, textarea, [role="button"]. A tile's root is a div role="group", so filmstrip tiles are invisible to it. Worth widening the selector regardless of which prototype wins.

The three-week-old bug underneath all of this — fixed

Before any redesign: the packer that arranges every gallery on every device was producing ragged rows. balancedRows reset its accumulator after each row while the threshold kept climbing, so only the first row could ever close. Asking for three rows of nine tiles returned two. Uniform grids were never generated, so the scorer never got to choose one.

1 · Screen share

The worst of the four, and the only one where the constraint is physical: a 16:9 share in a 375-wide portrait window can only ever be 202 px tall. The question isn't how to fit it — it's what to do with the other 449 px, and whether to keep the split at all.

S1

Share fills, roster collapses to a pill

ref: meet · zoom

Drop the vertical split entirely. The share takes the whole stage at its own aspect, centred, and the people become a single glass pill in the top-left — “4 others” — that expands into the existing People sheet. Same pixels for the share (202 px is 202 px), but the 449 px around it stops being black bars plus a roster and becomes headroom the share can actually use: pinch to zoom, and a double-tap fills the width in landscape.

The bet: during a share, faces are context, not content. Every reference app makes that bet on a phone. The pill keeps it one tap away rather than gone.

  • forRemoves the letterbox and the 12-slot roster in one move. No MIN_FRACTION special case.
  • forPinch-zoom becomes possible, which is what people actually want on a phone when the text is small.
  • againstLoses the “see the presenter's face while they talk” cue. A small speaker chip on the pill covers most of it.

S2

Let the fraction be honest

ref: teams

The smaller change: keep the split, but make MIN_FRACTION orientation-aware. In a vertical split the floor exists to stop the share becoming a sliver, and natural already guarantees that — the share never needs less than its own aspect demands. Drop the floor to ~0.34 for vertical and the share sits at 0.31 with no bars, the roster gets 400 px, and userRegionCapacity stops offering twelve slots to four people by also capping at the participant count.

  • forTwo constants and one clamp. Lowest-risk change in this document.
  • forKeeps faces and content on screen together, which is the one thing S1 gives up.
  • againstThe share is still 202 px tall. It removes the waste but doesn't make the share bigger — the physical limit is untouched.
  • againstNow the roster is the dominant region, which is the wrong emphasis during a presentation.

One feed. The strip becomes a swipe-up.

ref: meet · zoom · teams

No filmstrip on a phone. 3 · Small group — two to four

The one Manim mostly gets right. A 1-on-1 already routes to full-bleed-plus-card (phone1on1), and the corrected packer gives three and four people even rows. What's left is the wasted margin: tiles are centred in the stage with the island floating over the bottom, so a 2×2 leaves 87 px of dead space top and bottom while the tiles themselves stay at 176×234.

G1

Edge-to-edge tiles, island on glass

ref: facetime · whatsapp

Let the grid fill the stage instead of centring inside it, and let the island float over the bottom row the way it already floats over a full-bleed feed. Four tiles go from 176×234 to 176×318 — a 36% taller face — for the cost of the island covering the bottom ~60 px of two tiles, which is where a name pill sits anyway and which the auto-hide clears after four seconds.

  • forBiggest legibility win per line changed. The packer already fits-to-height; this only removes the centring and reserves nothing.
  • forMatches how the phone 1-on-1 and SoloStage already behave — the grid is the odd one out.
  • againstName pills on the bottom row need to move up out of the island's band, or duck with it.

G2

Prominence, not a layout switch

ref: facetime

FaceTime's move, shown on the right above: the speaker's tile grows where it is rather than the whole stage becoming a different layout. Below about six people this makes the grid/speaker toggle unnecessary — you get the benefit of speaker view without the mode. It's a weight applied to a row in the packer (a speaking tile counts for more summed aspect), not a new branch.

  • forRemoves a mode from a phone, and with it the swipe gesture that's blocking gallery paging in §4.
  • againstReflows on speech. The packer's whole stability story is that tiles don't move when people talk — this needs a debounce and a hard floor on how often it can change.

4 · Large group — twenty and up

Nine tiles per page on a phone, three pages for twenty-eight people, and no way to turn a page with your thumb: horizontal swipe is bound to grid ↔ speaker, so the pager is two arrow buttons floating at the vertical centre of the left and right edges — in the middle of the video, where a tap is also the chrome toggle. Nine 114×152 tiles is also more than a phone should try.

L1

Four per page, swipe and dots

ref: zoom · meet

Drop the phone page to four — 176×318 tiles, faces you can read — and page by horizontal swipe with a dot indicator on the shelf above the island, where the effects carousel already lives. This requires giving up the grid ↔ speaker swipe, which is the point: G2's prominence grid makes that mode redundant below six people, and above six a phone user wants pages, not modes. Layout switching stays in More and on the top chip, where it already is.

  • forFaces four times the area, the gesture every phone user already expects, and the arrows come off the video.
  • forFour mounted <video> per page instead of nine — the paging perf argument, more so.
  • againstSeven pages for 28 people. Needs the off-page-speaker jump to stay, and it becomes load-bearing rather than a nicety.
  • againstRebinds a shipped gesture. Anyone who learned swipe-to-switch loses it.

L2

Speaker plus three, and nobody counts pages

ref: teams large-meeting

The other reading of “20+”: at that size a gallery is a formality — you are watching whoever is talking, and the rest is a headcount. So don't page at all. The speaker takes a large tile, the two most recent speakers sit beneath it, and the fourth slot is +25 opening the People sheet. This is the presentation layout's OverflowTile pattern applied to the plain grid, and it's already built.

  • forThree mounted videos regardless of room size. By far the best on a phone's battery and decoder.
  • forNo pager, no gesture conflict, no “which page is Kofi on”.
  • againstYou can't browse the room visually. For a class or a standup where seeing everyone is the point, this is the wrong answer.
  • againstNeeds a recent-speaker history to fill the two small slots, which doesn't exist yet.

What all of them assume

  • 01The island needs a reserved band, or explicit permission to sit on glass. SoloStage reserves pb-24; GridStage and the speaker layout reserve nothing and collide by luck or don't. Pick one rule and apply it in both.
  • 02MIN_FRACTION has to know the split orientation. A floor that protects a horizontal split forces black bars in a vertical one. Same constant, opposite effect.
  • 03userRegionCapacity should cap at the participant count. Twelve slots for four people is what makes the current share layout feel empty as well as cramped.
  • 04The dead cols return needs resolving. gridCapacity computes a column cap the call site discards, so the phone's maxCols: 2 bounds page size but not columns — nine tiles render three across. Either feed it to the packer or delete it and fix the docstring.
  • 05Widen the overlap test's selector. It only sees button, a, input, select, textarea, [role="button"], and a tile is a div role="group" — which is why a filmstrip sitting 60 px under the control bar has never failed a gate.
  • 06Re-run the gates, and expect snapshots to move. test:mobile, test:mobile-sm, and 09-visual baselines. The packer fix alone changes desktop arrangement above four people.

If you want a recommendation

Land S2 + P1 + G1 first — they're three small, independent changes with no design risk between them, and together they're most of the complaint. S2 deletes 118 px of black bars by making one constant orientation-aware. P1 deletes a filmstrip that was two-thirds hidden and gives the focused feed 20% more height. G1 makes small-group faces 36% taller by removing a centring rule. None of them adds a layout branch, and none touches desktop.

Then L1, as the one real decision in here: four per page with swipe-and-dots is what every phone user already expects, but it costs the grid ↔ speaker swipe. I'd take that trade — the gesture is undiscoverable, it duplicates a control that exists in two other places, and G2 is the reason it stops mattering: once a speaker's tile grows in place, a small call doesn't need a speaker mode at all. G2 is the most interesting idea here and also the riskiest, because it reflows on speech, which is exactly the thing the packer's stability rules exist to prevent. It wants a debounce, a floor on change frequency, and its own prototype round.

S1 over S2 is worth revisiting once there's data on how often phone users actually rotate. It's the better answer to a shared screen on a phone and it's what Meet and Zoom both converged on — but it gives up seeing faces during a presentation, and that's a product call rather than a layout one. L2 I'd hold: it's the right shape for a 100-person webinar and the wrong one for a 12-person standup, and the room doesn't currently know which it is.
