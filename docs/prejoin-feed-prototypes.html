Manim · prejoin · layout study

The preview should be the page, not a stamp on it.

Today the camera feed is capped at 32dvh tall and 20rem wide, so on a 647 px window it renders about 230 × 172 px — roughly 10% of the card — while eight stacked rows of secondary chrome own the rest. Five ways to invert that, all keeping the feed at its true camera aspect.

The one job
Prejoin answers “what will everyone else see?” Every pixel not spent on that answer is overhead.
Hard constraint
Feed keeps the camera's real aspect — no crop, no bars. The box is the frame.
Hard constraint
No page scroll on prejoin, including mobile-sm. Growth has to come from the other rows.
Reference set
Meet (split), Zoom & WhatsApp (immersive), Teams (hero card).

Where the space actually goes

The feed isn't small because the cap is aggressive — it's small because nine siblings are queued below it and the cap exists to stop them being pushed off-screen. Any variant that only raises --pv-h re-introduces the scroll it was added to prevent. Each prototype instead removes or relocates rows so the feed can take the slack.

A

Split stage

ref: google meet

The card widens to max-w-4xl and splits: feed owns the left column at its native aspect, everything else stacks in a narrow right column. Vertical space stops being contested — the form's nine rows sit beside the feed rather than under it, so the feed can take the card's full height. Device toggles move onto the video as glass pills. Below md it collapses to one column with the feed still first and still full-width.

  • gainsFeed grows ~4× on desktop with zero rows removed — the least destructive change to existing content.
  • gainsReads instantly as a video product; matches the pattern most users already know from Meet.
  • costsTwo layouts to keep honest. The mobile branch gains nothing on its own — the phone still needs one of C/D's compressions.
  • costsA wide card on a short laptop can still overflow; the right column needs its own internal density pass.

B

Immersive stage

ref: zoom · whatsapp

Drop the card. The feed becomes the surface at its true aspect, centred on the stage, with a blurred, scaled copy of itself filling the leftover area so there are no dead bars at any ratio. Chrome floats: back and share as glass chips top-left/right, room name as a pill, and a single translucent island at the bottom carrying name, toggles and Join. Everything secondary — speaker test, low-bandwidth, E2EE, privacy — folds behind one “More options” disclosure in that island.

  • gainsLargest possible feed on every viewport, and identical in spirit phone-to-desktop — one layout, no branch.
  • gainsContinuous with the in-call screen: joining feels like stepping through, not switching pages.
  • costsBreaks the island model (§2 “nothing welded to the edge”) — needs an explicit STYLE.md carve-out, like the in-call stage has.
  • costsChrome-over-video means contrast is on --color-overlay, not a surface; the a11y gate has to be re-run on both themes.
  • costsCamera-off state has no video to sit on — needs a designed empty stage, not a grey box.

C

Hero card, condensed footer

ref: microsoft teams

Keep the card exactly where it is and attack the row count instead. The footer collapses from nine rows to three: mic level + device toggles + speaker test on one line, name field and Join side by side on the next, and everything remaining — encryption, low-bandwidth, privacy — onto a single meta line with the toggle inline. The feed then becomes flex-1 against the card's height instead of a fixed 32dvh: it takes whatever the footer doesn't.

  • gainsSmallest diff by far — no new layout branch, no STYLE.md exception, existing tests mostly hold.
  • gainsFixes the real bug: the cap disappears, so the feed grows on tall viewports instead of staying 32dvh forever.
  • costsCeiling is lower than A/B — on a wide desktop the card is still narrow, so the feed can only get so big.
  • costsName-and-Join on one row makes Join reachable before a name is typed; the disabled state has to read clearly.

D

Video-header card

ref: meet mobile · facetime

The feed bleeds to the card's own edges and becomes its header — full card width, rounded to the island's top corners, with a blurred cover fill behind the contained frame so no ratio ever shows bars. Room name, back and share overlay the video's top; device toggles overlay its bottom. What's left below is a genuinely short form: name, Join, one meta line. The card keeps its shape and shadow, so the island model survives intact.

  • gainsFeed gets the card's full width — on the 647 px window in the screenshot that alone is ~2.5× the current area.
  • gainsStays inside STYLE.md §2. No welded edges, no new surface rules, no a11y carve-out beyond the on-video chrome the app already ships.
  • costsThe blurred fill is a second decode of the same stream — cheap, but it's real GPU work on a low-end phone at exactly the moment we're about to connect.
  • costsRoom name on video is lower contrast than on a surface; long names need the overlay pill to truncate rather than wrap.

E

Aspect-adaptive

no direct ref — derived from the constraint

The others pick a layout from the viewport. This one picks it from the camera. previewAspect is already computed in PreJoin.tsx — feed it into the layout: a landscape camera (16:9, 4:3) stacks, taking the card's full width; a portrait camera (9:16, a phone held upright) pivots to side-by-side, because a tall frame leaves a natural column of space beside it that the form fills exactly. The frame is never letterboxed and never boxed in by a layout shaped for a ratio it doesn't have.

  • gainsDirectly answers the brief — “large enough while maintaining the aspect ratio” — for every camera, not just the common one.
  • gainsPortrait phone cameras currently waste the most space of any case; this is the only variant that recovers it.
  • costsLayout depends on an async stream read, so it can shift once when the ratio lands. Needs a transition or a held skeleton, or it's a jank source.
  • costsHardest to test — visual specs need a ratio matrix, and the camera-off state has no ratio to key off at all.
  • costsNovel. No reference app does this, so there's no borrowed familiarity to lean on.

What all five assume

These aren't variant-specific — they're the moves that make any of the five possible, and they're where most of the work actually is.

  • 01Delete the fixed --pv-h cap. It exists to stop nine rows overflowing. Once the rows shrink, the feed should size from remaining space, not a magic dvh.
  • 02Device toggles move onto the video. They're two 44 px targets currently eating a full row plus its gap — on video they cost nothing and read as more direct.
  • 03Secondary settings get demoted. Low-bandwidth, speaker test and the E2EE assurance are three full-width rows for things most people never touch. One meta line plus a “More” disclosure.
  • 04Aspect stays honest. No object-cover on the primary frame anywhere — the current comment in PreJoin.tsx is right, and cropping to fill would undo the whole point.
  • 05Re-run the vertical gates. Every variant needs mobile-sm checked for the no-scroll rule, and B and D need the a11y pass repeated for chrome sitting on video.

If you want a recommendation

D, with C's footer compression as the first commit. C alone is a small, safe change that fixes the actual defect — the fixed cap — and it's the prerequisite for every other variant, so it's worth landing on its own regardless of where this ends up. D then takes the freed space and gives the feed the card's full width without touching the island model, adding a layout branch, or opening a new a11y surface.

B is the most striking and would look best in a demo, but it welds chrome to the screen edge and moves every label onto --color-overlay — that's a STYLE.md amendment and a full a11y re-run, which is a lot to spend before we know the space problem is solved. A is the safe desktop answer but leaves mobile untouched. E is the most correct idea in the set and the one worth revisiting once D has shipped and we can see how often portrait cameras actually show up.
