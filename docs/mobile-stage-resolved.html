The thing your picks unlock

I'd called the swipe rebinding “the one real trade” — take horizontal swipe for gallery paging and you lose grid ↔ speaker switching. Putting “one feed” and “swipe + dots” together removes the trade entirely, because it's Zoom's actual model: speaker view isn't a mode you toggle into, it's the first page of one horizontal sequence. Swipe left from the speaker and you're on gallery page one. There is nothing to switch, so nothing is lost.

Three consequences worth naming, because they're all simplifications:

  • 01The swipe-up roster peek I proposed is unnecessary. The roster is the next page. One fewer gesture to teach, and the dots advertise it — which the swipe never did.
  • 02FaceTime-style grow-in-place prominence can be dropped. I'd recommended it to make the speaker mode redundant on small calls; page 0 does that for free, without reflowing tiles on speech — the thing the packer's stability rules exist to prevent.
  • 03It fixes a discoverability hole I hadn't found. LayoutChip.tsx — whose own docstring calls it “the single layout affordance on every device”, and which ControlBar's comments point at as “the top chip” — is never imported anywhere. It's dead code. So today the only routes to switch layout on a phone are the undiscoverable swipe and More → View. Page 0 turns the swipe into the primary, signposted route.

Verdict on each pick

adopt Full-screen share with a collapsible roster strip

Better than either of my share prototypes, and for a reason I'd missed: in portrait the share is width-bound, not height-bound. A 16:9 share in a 359-wide column paints 359×202 and can never be taller, so the remaining 449 px is free. My S1 spent it on nothing (a pill); my S2 spent it keeping a split that forced 118 px of black bars. A strip costs the share literally zero pixels.

The dynamic rule you asked for falls out of that. The strip is free exactly while the share doesn't need the height — so gate it on the share's painted height, not on orientation or headcount. A 16:9 share leaves 449 px, an ultrawide 21:9 leaves 497 px, a 4:3 leaves 382 px — all room for a strip. A portrait-shaped share (a phone window, a document) paints 479 px tall and leaves 172 px: strip auto-collapses to its handle. In landscape the share becomes height-bound (583×328 of a 651×359 stage) and the strip becomes an edge rail or collapses. Same rule, no special cases.

One thing to get right: “collapsible” needs a handle that never disappears. If the strip collapses to nothing, or collapses along with the auto-hiding chrome, you've rebuilt the orphaned state in a different place — a roster that exists but has no reachable way back.

adopt One feed in speaker view

Unchanged from P1, and the measurements back it hard: the filmstrip it removes is 96 px tall with 60 px of it underneath the control island and 8 px under the self-view card. It was two-thirds invisible. The focused feed goes from 543 px to the full 651 px.

amend 2×2 up to 3×3 — gate it on width, not headcount

“Up to 3×3” reads as denser when there are more people. It has to be denser when there's more screen, because the codebase already declares its own floor: gridCapacity sets minW: 132 for coarse pointers. Three columns needs 412 px of stage. Here is what you actually get:

Your 3×3 ceiling, driven by width, gives it 245 px tiles nine at a time. Your pick is worth adopting partly for a device neither of us was thinking about.

amend Corner card — keep it draggable

Teams', Meet's and Discord's corner cards are all draggable. The standard is “starts in a corner, can be moved”, and the reason is concrete: a fixed card covers whatever it happens to cover — a shared spreadsheet's totals column, someone's face — with no escape.

What's actually wrong with today's card isn't that it moves, it's that it moves badly. useDraggable clamps to the viewport but has no corner snapping and no persistence: release mid-edge and it stays mid-edge, rotate the phone and it lands somewhere arbitrary, and every remount resets it. The real “corner card” is snap-to-nearest-corner on release, with the chosen corner remembered — a smaller change than making it static, and it's what the pick was reaching for.

And it collides with the strip. During a share, a floating self card and a roster strip both want the bottom band, and at 375 px one will cover the other. Proposal: while someone is sharing, self joins the strip as its first item — you're a participant like anyone else, the floating card is redundant, and that's one fewer overlay on the smallest screen. It's also what Teams does. Note this means the self-view changes representation as you swipe (corner card on page 0, a tile in the gallery, first item in the strip during a share). Zoom does exactly that too, so it's familiar — but it's a decision, not an accident, so it's written here.

Two things your combination doesn't resolve

needs a call Dots stop working past about five pages

28 people at four per page is seven pages. iOS page controls compress past roughly eight dots and stop communicating anything; nobody swipes seven times to find a colleague either. Above ~5 pages the indicator should become a Speaker · 3/7 pill rather than dots, and the existing off-page-speaker jump stops being a nicety and becomes the primary way to reach someone.

Also: the dots should not hide with the auto-hiding chrome. They're status, not control — same category as CallStatusBar. Hiding them takes away your sense of where you are in the sequence for no benefit.

needs a call When does a gallery stop being worth browsing?

This is a product question about Manim's calls, not a layout one, so I'm flagging rather than deciding. If a 20+ call is a class — seeing everyone is the point — pages are right and the answer is a better indicator. If it's a standup — you watch whoever is talking — then above some threshold page 1 should end in a +N overflow tile opening the People sheet, and the later pages shouldn't exist. The OverflowTile for that is already built and used by the presentation layout.

My instinct is a threshold rather than a choice: page through up to ~3 pages, overflow beyond that. But it depends on what your calls actually are, and I'd rather ask than guess.

Device picker — confirmed, one detail to pin

B's tray shell, A's flat route-first content, C's rail as the collapsed state. Two notes from composing them, neither a conflict:

  • 01C's collapsed chip must not resize. A chip that reads the device name — “AirPods”, “Jabra Evolve2 85” — changes width when the route changes, which moves the buttons on either side of it. That's the moving-target problem the audit was complaining about, reintroduced. Fix: fixed-width chip with truncation, or icon-plus-route-glyph. It's shown fixed-width in the frames above.
  • 02The tray holds routes; the long tail needs a door. Routes are bounded — four at most — so B's tray fits them without scrolling. A laptop with six microphones doesn't fit, so the tray's last row is “All devices”, which opens A as a full sheet. That's the two composing rather than competing.

What I'd build, in what order

One. The pager: speaker as page 0, gallery pages after it, swipe plus an indicator, and width-derived columns (2 below 430 px, 3 above, capped at your 3×3). This is the spine — the share layout and the self-view both hang off it — and it retires the dead LayoutChip along with the undiscoverable swipe-to-switch.

Two. The share: full-bleed at its own aspect, roster strip gated on the share's painted height, self as the strip's first item, persistent handle. Independent of everything else and it deletes the worst number in the audit (a shared screen getting 31% of the display).

Three. Corner-snapping and persistence for the self-view card, and drop the filmstrip from speaker view. Both small; the filmstrip removal is a deletion.

Then the device picker tray, which is a new primitive and wants the a11y pass repeated — it holds focusable controls while the stage is still live, which is the exact shape that produced the aria-hidden-focus finding behind DropdownMenu's non-modal note.

Two answers needed before I start: is the 132 px legibility floor staying (which decides whether 3-up appears on a 375 px phone), and are 20+ calls browsable (pages) or not (overflow tile).
