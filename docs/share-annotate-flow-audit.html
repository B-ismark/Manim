Manim · in-call · flow audit

Three reported symptoms, one architectural cause, and seven more waiting behind it.

A read of the share and annotate paths across Stage.tsx, AnnotationOverlay.tsx, useSharePresence.ts, focusTrack.ts, wire.ts and the four places that start a screen share. Every finding below cites the line it came from; nothing here is inferred from behaviour alone.

The reported three

Two of them are the same bug seen from different angles. The app decides whether to show you your own screen share purely from “is anyone else sharing?” — it never asks what kind of surface you picked, even though the browser tells it.

Two crosshairs while annotating One follows your hand; the other lags behind it inside the video.
→
You are watching your own monitor capture The capture bakes the OS cursor into the frame. Since the CSS cursor is crosshair while the pen is armed, the crosshair is captured, re-encoded, and painted back under the real one — offset by the mirror’s scale and a round trip of latency.
Infinite mirror on full-screen share The stage shows the stage showing the stage.
→
Same line, same omission Stage.tsx:281 renders your own share whenever no one else is sharing. The comment four lines above already says the echo “only recurses when sharing a whole monitor” — but displaySurface is never read anywhere in src/.
Sharing a window jumps you to the window You never see the presentation layout, so the pen is two window-switches away.
→
No CaptureController is created Chrome’s default for window and tab capture is focus-captured-surface: it brings the captured surface to the front. The Conditional Focus API exists to opt out of exactly this, and the app never opts out.

Start-a-share, as it runs today

Current — window or tab share

Tap Share→ Picker→ Pick a window→ Chrome focuses that window→ Manim is behind it→ Annotate never seen

The presentation layout is built correctly underneath — PresentationStage puts the share in the big region with everyone tiled beside it, and AnnotateControl is pinned to the share tile. The presenter simply isn’t looking at the page any more when it happens.

Current — entire-screen share

Tap Share→ Pick “Entire screen”→ Own share fills the big region→ Mirror tunnel→ Arm pen→ Cursor captured + drawn twice

The self-view was deliberately turned on so annotation would be discoverable (Stage.tsx:263–281) — a sound call for a window share, where nothing can recurse. It was applied to every surface type, which is where it breaks.

Proposed — one branch on displaySurface, one controller

Tap Share→ Picker, Manim tab excluded→ Read displaySurface→ no-focus-change→ Stay on the presentation layout
window / tab→ Live share in the big region + pen monitor→ Presenter card, no self-echo, pen disabled with a reason

“Switch to a different app”, translated to the web: we can’t move the OS focus, but we can move the stage off the share — which is the part that actually causes the tunnel and the doubled cursor. Teams and Slack reach the same end state by minimising their own window; the observable result for the presenter is identical.

Findings

Ten, ordered by how much they cost a real call. F1–F3 are the reported three; F4–F10 came out of reading the paths around them.

F1

The armed pen shows two cursors

blocker
Evidence
  • src/islands/AnnotationOverlay.tsx:130 — canvas gets cursor-crosshair while armed
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:281 — the presenter is shown their own share
Mechanism
Chrome captures the OS pointer into the display stream by default. Arming the pen changes the OS pointer to a crosshair, so the crosshair is what gets captured. The presenter then sees the real pointer and its captured copy — the second one scaled by the mirror and delayed by an encode/decode round trip, which is why it reads as two separate cursors rather than one blurry one.
Fix
Resolving F2 removes the duplicate on monitor shares (there is no self-echo left to carry it). Separately, replace the generic crosshair with a pen-tip cursor in the author’s own palette colour: armed state becomes unambiguous, and if a captured copy ever appears again it is visibly the pen, not a second pointer.
Corrected
This audit originally ruled out constraining the capture with cursor: 'never', calling it a legacy constraint with uneven support. That was wrong, and testing rather than reading the API surface is what caught it: getSupportedConstraints().cursor reports false, but Chromium honours the constraint anyway — applyConstraints({cursor:'never'}) resolves on a live track and getSettings() reports it back. The second objection was sound though, so the shipped version is scoped: the captured pointer is suppressed only while the pen is armed and handed straight back on disarm. Viewers still follow the presenter’s pointer during a walkthrough; during a stroke the ink says what the arrow would, in an attributed colour.
F2

Own-share self-view ignores the surface type

blocker
Evidence
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:281 — showOwnShare = Boolean(localScreenShare) && !remoteSharing
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:279 — comment: “The echo only recurses when sharing a whole monitor”
  • grep -rn "displaySurface" src/ → no hits
Mechanism
The recursion condition is written down in the comment and never expressed in code. track.getSettings().displaySurface returns 'monitor' | 'window' | 'browser' and is available the moment the capture starts.
Fix
Read displaySurface at publish time into the room store, then branch the big region: window/browser keeps today’s behaviour (live share, pen available — nothing can recurse); monitor replaces the live tile with a presenter card (“You’re sharing your entire screen” + Stop sharing + what viewers can see), and hides the pen with a stated reason rather than a dead button.
Also
Pass selfBrowserSurface: 'exclude' so the Manim tab can’t be picked at all, and surfaceSwitching: 'include' so a presenter can change what they’re sharing without stopping and restarting. Both are typed options on ScreenShareCaptureOptions in livekit-client 2.9.5.
F3

Capture steals window focus, so the pen is never found

blocker
Evidence
  • grep -rn "CaptureController" src/ → no hits
  • livekit-client@2.9.5 options.d.ts:175 — controller?: unknown is accepted and forwarded
Mechanism
For window and tab captures Chrome defaults to focus-captured-surface and raises the captured surface. The presenter lands in the shared window, not in Manim, so the presentation layout and the Annotate button on the share tile are behind another window at the exact moment they’d be used.
Fix
Create a CaptureController, pass it through ScreenShareCaptureOptions.controller, and call setFocusBehavior('no-focus-change') so the presenter stays on the call.
Risk
Needs verification. Conditional Focus must be invoked within the microtask in which the capture promise settles, and LiveKit awaits getDisplayMedia internally — so the call may land too late. Fallback if it does: capture with getDisplayMedia directly in our own hook and publish the resulting track, which also gives us displaySurface at the same point (F2) and one place to own capture options (F4).
F4

Four entry points start a share; none share options

high
Evidence
  • src/islands/ControlBar.tsx:323 — More-sheet tile (touch)
  • src/islands/ControlBar.tsx:591 — control-bar button (desktop)
  • src/islands/PipPanel.tsx:123 — mini-player button
  • src/features/calls/useMediaDeviceWatch.ts:94 — the “Share again” toast action
Mechanism
All four call localParticipant.setScreenShareEnabled(...) bare. Every fix above adds capture options — and would apply to whichever call sites we remembered to edit. The toast is the one that gets missed: it’s the recovery path, so a share restarted after an accidental stop would silently lose the focus controller and the surface-type read.
Fix
One useScreenShare() hook that owns the options object, the controller, the displaySurface read and the store write. The four call sites become start() / stop(). This is the change that makes F2 and F3 implementable once rather than four times.
F5

Ink carries no target — two shares means strokes land on the wrong screen

high
Evidence
  • src/lib/annotate/wire.ts:21–25 — header is version, colorIdx, strokeId, seq. No track id.
  • src/lib/annotate/geometry.ts — coordinates are unit-space against whatever is in the big region
  • src/lib/focusTrack.ts:20–24 — primaryShare() re-sorts on participant.isSpeaking
Mechanism
A stroke means “65% across, 30% down the featured share”, and the featured share is chosen by who is currently talking. With two people presenting, the big region swaps as they take turns — and live ink, which lasts ~4s, re-lands on a different screen entirely. Receivers can’t detect it: the packet doesn’t say what it was drawn on.
Fix
Two parts. Put the target track SID in the packet header (bump WIRE_VERSION; drop strokes whose target isn’t the currently featured share) — and make primaryShare() sticky: once a share is featured it stays featured until it ends or someone explicitly switches. Speaking is a bad reason to re-aim a shared drawing surface.
F6

Spotlighting a person orphans the stroke in progress

high
Evidence
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:756–760 — the overlay mounts only while bigIsShare
  • src/islands/AnnotationOverlay.tsx:36–41 — unmount calls engine.detach()
  • src/store/useAnnotateStore.ts — active is untouched by any of this
Mechanism
Spotlight someone mid-stroke and the canvas unmounts under the pointer. endLocal() never runs, so the stroke never gets its final flush; the pen stays armed in the store with nothing to draw on, and silently re-arms when the spotlight clears — the next click starts drawing with no announcement and no visible mode change.
Fix
End any live local stroke in the overlay’s cleanup, and disarm the pen whenever the featured tile stops being a share — the same disarm that already exists for “the last share ended” (ControlBar.tsx:122–124), extended to cover this case.
F7

Three hand-picked offsets stack in one corner of the share tile

high
Evidence
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:1239 — tile action at right-2 top-2
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:608 — fullscreen at right-2 top-14
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:639 — annotate at right-2 top-[6.5rem]
Mechanism
Three siblings independently choosing a vertical offset in the same column, each assuming the other two’s sizes. This is precisely the failure TopStack was introduced to eliminate at screen level (CLAUDE.md: “never a new fixed + hand-picked z-index”), reproduced one level down inside the tile. On a short big region — the vertical split on a phone, or a share demoted into a grid cell — the third button runs into the name pill at the bottom.
Fix
A single TileActionStack that lays the column out with gap and takes children in priority order, exactly as TopStack does. Adding a fourth control then costs nothing.
F8

The pen appears when a share exists, not when one is featured

medium
Evidence
  • src/islands/ControlBar.tsx:113–114 — someoneSharing = shareTracks.length > 0
  • src/islands/Stage.tsx:756 — the canvas mounts only when the big tile is a share
Mechanism
Spotlight a person, or demote the share to the grid, and the control-bar pen is still enabled — arming it toggles a store flag with no canvas anywhere. The Announcer even says “Annotation on. Draw on the shared screen.” to a screen-reader user who now has no surface at all.
Fix
Derive the pen’s availability from the same value the canvas mounts on. useSharePresence() is already the shared-truth hook for exactly this class of disagreement — add “a share is currently featured” to it and read it in both places.
F9

Touch users see ink appear with no explanation

medium
Evidence
  • src/islands/AnnotationOverlay.tsx:34 — canDraw = active && allowed && !touch
  • src/islands/ControlBar.tsx — the pen control is hidden entirely on touch
Mechanism
View-only on touch is the right v1 call and is well argued in the code. But a phone user watching strokes bloom and fade over a shared screen gets no label, no control, and no reason — the author name is drawn beside the live stroke head only while it’s alive, which on a small screen is easy to miss.
Fix
A one-shot pill the first time remote ink arrives on a touch device: “Name is drawing on the shared screen”. Reuses the existing announcement the Announcer already emits — this just gives it a visible counterpart.
F10

Shared text is encoded as motion, and contentHint can’t fix it on the desktop path — withdrawn, the premise was wrong

withdrawn
Evidence
  • src/lib/livekit.ts:62 — desktop publishes vp9
  • livekit-client — for an SVC codec on a ScreenShare track it forces mediaStreamTrack.contentHint = 'motion' and scalabilityMode = 'L1T3', overriding whatever the caller passed
  • and, five lines of comment later: getDefaultDegradationPreference() returns 'maintain-resolution' for Track.Source.ScreenShare — its own comment says this exists “since we are overriding contentHint to motion … it overrides the default degradationPreference to balanced”
What I got wrong
The stomp is real; the harm attributed to it is not. The motion hint’s consequence — dropping resolution before frame rate — is already cancelled by LiveKit, deliberately, and src/lib/livekit.ts:68 sets the same maintain-resolution explicitly on top. I read the first half of the mechanism and stopped before the half that compensates for it.
Why the proposed fix was backwards
Publishing the share as VP8 would lose VP9’s efficiency (VP8 is worse on text at equal bitrate), and drop the track out of the SVC path into simulcast: true, adding a second ~540p layer that spends uplink on nothing. Setting contentHint: 'detail' while keeping VP9 is worse still — the L1T3 workaround the stomp exists to avoid caps Chrome at ~5 fps. Both directions regress.
Standing recommendation
Ship nothing. If a share ever does look soft, the lever is screenShareEncoding / screenShareSimulcastLayers in publishDefaults — never the codec. Unverified either way without real bandwidth measurement: VP9-vs-VP8 sharpness at these bitrates, and whether ~4 Mbps is ever the binding constraint.

Suggested order

  1. Land useScreenShare() first (F4)

    Nothing else can be done once until there is one place that starts a share. It’s a pure refactor with no behaviour change, so it can go in and be verified on its own.

  2. Surface-type branch + focus controller (F2, F3)

    Both live inside the new hook. This is the commit that kills the mirror tunnel, the doubled cursor on monitor shares, and the focus jump — the user-visible fix for all three reports.

  3. Pen-tip cursor and mode clarity (F1, F8)

    Small, and best done while the same code is open: a distinctive armed cursor, and a pen that is only offered when there is a canvas under it.

  4. Annotation correctness (F5, F6)

    Sticky primaryShare, target SID on the wire, stroke ended on detach. F5 bumps the wire version, so it wants its own commit and its own multi-party check against a local server.

  5. Tile action stack (F7), then the polish item (F9)

    F7 is a primitive extraction that pays for itself the next time anything is added to a tile corner. F10 was listed last here because it needed a bandwidth comparison; that comparison turned out to be unnecessary — reading the rest of the mechanism withdrew the finding entirely.

What this audit could not verify

The LiveKit gates are frozen and this environment has no installed dependency tree, so nothing below was executed. Findings are read from source and from livekit-client@2.9.5’s published types and bundle, both of which were fetched and read directly rather than recalled.

Needs a run against a local livekit-server before any of this ships: whether Conditional Focus fires early enough through LiveKit’s own await (F3 — this decides between passing a controller and taking over the capture ourselves); whether the monitor-share presenter card reads as intentional rather than broken; and the multi-party stroke-target behaviour in F5, which by definition needs two publishers.
